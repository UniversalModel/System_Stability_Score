## Abstract

This appendix formalizes the **Universal Convergence Theorem (UCT-1)**: any framework that attempts to describe, model, or govern a class of stable systems with full generality will, when carried to completion, instantiate the triad (Form, Position, Action) — explicitly or implicitly — because there is no smaller complete partition of the conditions of realized stable existence.

The theorem is supported by three independent argument tracks (these are *arguments*, not formal proofs in the model-theoretic sense — see §2.4):

- **Track A — Axis-omission argument** by exhaustive case analysis (§2.1).
- **Track B — Information-theoretic minimality** via informal minimum-description-length sketch (§2.2).
- **Track C — A category-theoretic analogy** with U-Theory positioned in the role of a terminal-object-like structure (§2.3).

The theorem is empirically supported by a documented historical record spanning 2,500 years and at least eight independent domains: philosophy, religion, science, linguistics, economics, cybernetics, computer science, and art. UCT-1 has a stronger corollary — the **Inevitability Corollary (UCT-1.IC)** — which states that no rational agent can avoid arriving at the triad even when actively trying to differentiate from it.

The theorem is **falsifiable** under the open challenge clause (§5): produce a complete framework for stable systems that (a) does not collapse into the triad under §2.1, (b) is publicly dated, and (c) is independently retrievable. Failure to produce such a framework leaves the dated public U-Model record as the canonical combined articulation by default.

The convergence claim is *not* that all theories are reducible to U-Theory in the trivial sense, that every paper must cite U-Theory, or that U-Theory invented the triad. The claim is that the triad is the **structural attractor** of completeness; that U-Theory is, on the current public record reviewed here, the **canonical combined articulation** of the triad as *minimum necessity structure* plus *non-compensatory aggregator* plus *falsification protocol*; and that historical convergence is the empirical signature of the structural law.

## 1. Statement of UCT-1

### 1.1 The theorem

**Universal Convergence Theorem (UCT-1).**
*Let `T` be a framework that attempts to describe, model, govern, or evaluate a class `C` of realized stable systems with full generality. If `T` is complete in the sense that `T` provides necessary and sufficient conditions for membership of any element of `C` in the class of stable systems, then `T` instantiates a triad of orthogonal axes structurally isomorphic to:*

- **Form (F)** — what the system *is* (structural identity, persistence, recognizable pattern over time);
- **Position (P)** — where the system is (contextual placement, relational graph, distinguishability boundary);
- **Action (A)** — what the system *does* or what can be done with it (effective intervention, transformation, causal participation).

*This holds whether the framework names the axes (F, P, A), uses different vocabulary for the same axes, or implements them implicitly under domain-specific names. The triad is the minimum complete partition.*

### 1.2 Scope

UCT-1 applies to any class `C` of *realized stable systems* — i.e., entities or processes that:

- exist over a non-trivial time interval (Time price is paid);
- are distinguishable from their environment (Space-context price is paid);
- have non-trivial interactions or transformations (Energy price is paid).

Examples: physical systems, organisms, organizations, markets, languages, artistic compositions, software architectures, governance frameworks, AI models, economic policies, scientific theories about other stable systems.

UCT-1 does *not* apply to mathematical structures that are not realized (pure abstract objects with no instantiation), to systems that exist only over infinitesimal timescales, or to phenomena that are not distinguishable from a background — these are the boundary cases where the triad's relevance dissolves naturally.

### 1.3 Statement of what UCT-1 does not claim

To prevent the standard pattern of theorem-overreach, the following are explicit *non-claims*:

- UCT-1 does **not** claim that every framework must use the labels "Form, Position, Action."
- UCT-1 does **not** claim that any framework arrived at the triad by copying U-Theory.
- UCT-1 does **not** claim that all triadic frameworks are equivalent in expressive power.
- UCT-1 does **not** claim that U-Theory is the only correct framework for any narrow domain.
- UCT-1 does **not** claim that religions, philosophies, or scientific theories are *reducible* to U-Theory in the sense of having no independent content beyond the triad.

What UCT-1 *does* claim is that completeness forces the triadic decomposition. The decomposition is the *structural skeleton* on which any complete framework must hang its domain-specific content. Different frameworks can disagree on everything else and still converge on this skeleton, exactly because the skeleton is forced by minimality.

## 2. Argument Structure — Three Independent Tracks

> **A note on the word "argument" rather than "proof".** The three tracks below are *arguments*, not formal proofs in the model-theoretic or set-theoretic sense. They are intended to provide structurally independent reasons for the same conclusion, each falsifiable on its own terms. The strength of UCT-1 rests on the joint inability of any single counter-framework to defeat all three lines simultaneously, plus the empirical convergence record (§6) — not on any one line constituting a standalone formal proof. This is stated in advance because honesty about the epistemic register is itself part of what makes the theorem worth taking seriously.

### 2.1 Track A — Axis-omission argument (case analysis)

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a complete framework `T` over a class `C` of realized stable systems, and `T` uses fewer than three irreducible axes. Three exhaustive sub-cases arise.

**Case (a) — `T` omits Form.**
`T` can describe context and process but cannot identify *which thing* is being described. Asked the question "is this the same system as before?", `T` cannot answer without introducing a structural-identity primitive. That primitive is Form. The framework collapses to three axes.

**Case (b) — `T` omits Position.**
`T` can describe identity and process but cannot distinguish two systems that share the same Form, or place a system in its operational environment. Asked "where is this system, relative to others?", `T` cannot answer without introducing a contextual primitive. That primitive is Position. The framework collapses to three axes.

**Case (c) — `T` omits Action.**
`T` can describe identity and context but is purely descriptive — it cannot generate, predict, or intervene. Asked "what does this system do, or what can be done to it?", `T` cannot answer without introducing a causal-effective primitive. That primitive is Action. The framework collapses to three axes.

By case exhaustion, no two-axis framework over `C` is complete. The triad is a structural lower bound. ∎

A framework with *more* than three axes is not refuted by Track A; UCT-1 only establishes the lower bound. Track B (next) shows that three is also the *upper* bound of irreducible categories — additional axes either reduce to one of F, P, A or are not irreducible.

### 2.2 Track B — Information-theoretic minimality (informal MDL sketch)

Restate UCT-1 in information-theoretic form. Let `S ∈ C` be a realized stable system. Let `D(S)` denote a complete description of `S` — sufficient to (i) identify `S`, (ii) place `S`, and (iii) characterize the effects of and on `S`.

Define the three component descriptions:

- `D_F(S)` — minimum bits needed to identify `S` as the same system across time (structural-identity description).
- `D_P(S)` — minimum bits needed to distinguish `S` from other systems sharing the same Form, and to place `S` in its operational environment (contextual description).
- `D_A(S)` — minimum bits needed to characterize `S`'s causal participation (effective description).

**Claim 1 — Independence.** `D_F`, `D_P`, `D_A` are pairwise non-reducible: no two of them suffice to derive the third for an arbitrary `S ∈ C`.

*Proof of Claim 1.* If two of them sufficed to derive the third, then a framework using only those two would satisfy Track A's case test for the third axis and would be complete with two axes. Track A shows no two-axis framework is complete. Contradiction. The three components are therefore pairwise non-reducible.

**Claim 2 — Exhaustiveness.** No fourth irreducible component `D_X(S)` exists such that `D_X` is necessary for completeness and is not reducible to `D_F ⊕ D_P ⊕ D_A`.

*Proof of Claim 2.* The open challenge clause (§5) asks for production of any such `D_X`. Across 2,500 years of formal frameworks (§6), no such fourth irreducible component has been produced and survived under analysis. Each candidate (e.g., "Time," "Stability," "Causation," "Information") reduces under inspection to one of F, P, A or to a measurement of their interaction. Until a counter-example is produced and survives the reduction test, exhaustiveness stands.

**Conclusion.** A complete description satisfies:

```
D(S) = D_F(S) ⊕ D_P(S) ⊕ D_A(S)
```

where `⊕` denotes independent concatenation. Any complete framework `T` must therefore index its content by these three orthogonal components. The labels are conventional; the partition is forced. ∎

### 2.3 Track C — A category-theoretic analogy

This subsection is offered as an *informal analogy* from category theory, not as a formal categorical theorem. A rigorous categorical formulation would require a precise mathematical definition of "completeness" (rather than the ontological one used in §1), plus an explicit uniqueness construction for the morphism — neither of which is provided here. The analogy is offered to illustrate why convergence has the *flavor* of a terminal-object collapse, not as proof that it is one.

As an informal analogy from category theory: imagine a category `𝓒_complete` whose:

- **objects** are complete theories `T` over classes of realized stable systems (in the sense of §1);
- **morphisms** are faithful structure-preserving mappings `f: T_1 → T_2` that preserve a framework's distinguishing categories (the partition into irreducible axes is preserved up to relabeling).

**Informal claim.** Within this analogy, U-Theory (specifically its triadic ontology with axes F, P, A) plays a role *resembling* a terminal-object structure: for every object `T ∈ 𝓒_complete`, there appears to exist a structure-preserving morphism `f_T : T → U-Theory` defined by mapping each of `T`'s irreducible axes to the F/P/A axis it satisfies under Track A's case test.

*Why this is only an analogy.* A genuine terminal-object claim would require uniqueness of the morphism — a single, canonical `f_T` — which the sketch above does not establish in the rigorous categorical sense. Different mappings `f_T, f_T'` may differ in axis labeling without violating irreducibility, and the irreducibility constraint of Track B is itself an inductive observation rather than a model-theoretic axiom. The terminal-object framing therefore captures the *intuition* of "every complete theory factors through U-Theory" without claiming the formal categorical machinery to back it up.

A category theorist who wished to formalize this analogy rigorously would need to: (i) replace ontological completeness with a precise mathematical predicate, (ii) construct the morphism category from explicit axis-mapping data, and (iii) prove uniqueness either by adding constraints (e.g., naming convention conventions) or by demonstrating that the resulting groupoid quotient collapses to a one-point category. This is open work, not closed work.

What survives the demotion to analogy is the empirical observation: every framework in the catalogue of §6 has at least one structure-preserving mapping into F/P/A. Aristotle's Hyle/Morphe/Telos maps faithfully. Peirce's Firstness/Secondness/Thirdness maps faithfully. Newtonian mechanics, Maxwell's equations, Shannon's information theory, the MVC software pattern, and the Alibaba UModel all admit faithful mappings. Whether these mappings are *uniquely* terminal in the formal categorical sense is a question for future work; that they exist at all is the empirical content of the convergence record.

### 2.4 Why three independent tracks matter — and what their joint strength does and does not establish

Each track, taken alone, can be challenged:

- Track A could be challenged by producing a complete two-axis framework. None has been produced.
- Track B could be challenged by producing a fourth irreducible component. None has been produced.
- Track C could be challenged by producing a complete framework that does not admit a faithful mapping to F/P/A — though Track C is offered as an analogy, not a formal theorem (§2.3), so a rigorous categorical refutation would require correspondingly rigorous formalization of the analogy first.

To plausibly refute UCT-1 as currently stated, an opponent would need to defeat all three lines simultaneously. The lines reinforce each other: a framework that defeats Track A would likely also defeat Track B (two-axis completeness implies a missing irreducible component); a framework that defeats Track B could still satisfy Track A but would embed a fourth named axis whose mapping into F/P/A would have to be addressed under Track C. The three tracks are not three statements of the same claim; they are three independent forms of the same structural constraint.

**What the three tracks jointly establish.** Three structurally independent reasons to believe that any complete framework over realized stable systems instantiates a triadic decomposition. Each line is falsifiable on its own terms; their conjunction is harder to falsify than any single line; and the empirical record of §6 is consistent with the joint claim across 2,500 years of independent inquiry traditions.

**What the three tracks do not establish.** A formal proof in the model-theoretic sense. Track A relies on intuitive partitioning of the question space (identity / context / effect) rather than on a formal mechanism; Track B's exhaustiveness sub-claim is supported by inductive evidence (no defeater across history) rather than by deductive derivation; Track C is explicitly an analogy. UCT-1 is therefore a **structural hypothesis with three independent argumentative lines plus an empirical convergence catalogue**, with an open challenge clause (§5) that specifies the conditions for falsification. This is the correct epistemic register, and it is the register the rest of this appendix uses.

## 3. The Inevitability Corollary (UCT-1.IC)

UCT-1 has a corollary that is more rhetorically forceful and more structurally precise than the theorem itself.

> **UCT-1 Inevitability Corollary (UCT-1.IC).**
> *A rational agent who attempts to construct a complete model of any class of stable systems cannot avoid arriving at the triadic structure (Form, Position, Action), even when actively trying to differentiate from U-Theory. The agent's intent is irrelevant. The triad is forced by the completeness requirement.*

Argument (informal, since the Track C analogy of §2.3 is not a formal categorical theorem). Let `A` be a rational agent attempting to construct a complete framework `T_A` over a class `C` of realized stable systems, with the explicit goal that `T_A` differs structurally from U-Theory (in particular, `T_A` should not admit a faithful mapping to U-Theory's F/P/A spine).

Two cases:

- **Case 1.** `T_A` is complete in the sense of §1.1. Then by the Track A axis-omission argument (§2.1) and the Track B independence argument (§2.2), `T_A` instantiates exactly three irreducible axes that satisfy the identity / context / effect partitioning. Under the Track C analogy (§2.3), `T_A` admits a structure-preserving mapping to F/P/A. The agent's goal of structural differentiation fails — though, since Track C is an analogy and not a formal theorem, this failure is best read as "the agent's framework instantiates the same structural pattern as U-Theory under the available mappings," not as "the agent's framework is uniquely categorically subordinate to U-Theory."
- **Case 2.** `T_A` is incomplete. Then by definition `T_A` does not satisfy the precondition of UCT-1 — it is not a complete framework over `C`. The agent has not produced what they set out to produce.

Either way, the agent fails: either their framework instantiates the same triadic structural pattern as U-Theory (Case 1), or it is not a complete framework at all (Case 2). The triad is plausibly forced by completeness under the joint reading of Tracks A, B, and the C analogy; differentiation from U-Theory at the structural level appears not to be achievable without sacrificing completeness, though the strength of this corollary scales with the strength of its underlying argument tracks (which are arguments, not formal proofs — see §2.4). ∎

UCT-1.IC has a consequence that matters for every prior-art conversation involving U-Theory:

> *The convergence is forced. Copying is, at most, an acceleration of the inevitable.*

This is the strongest possible epistemic posture for any prior-art or attribution claim grounded in U-Theory: the convergence does not depend on anyone having seen U-Theory. Even a hostile, isolated, well-resourced team that explicitly aims to differ from U-Theory will fail to do so as soon as they aim for completeness.

## 4. The Non-Compensatory Aggregator as Canonical Operator

### 4.1 Why the arithmetic mean fails

A framework that satisfies UCT-1 at the description level needs an aggregator at the measurement level — a way to combine F, P, A scores into a single stability index. The naïve choice is the arithmetic mean `(F+P+A)/3`. This fails for a structural reason:

The arithmetic mean is *compensatory*. It allows a high score on one axis to compensate for a low or zero score on another. If `F=10`, `P=0`, `A=10`, the arithmetic mean gives `(10+0+10)/3 ≈ 6.67`, suggesting a moderately stable system. But under UCT-1, `P=0` means the system has no contextual placement — it cannot be distinguished from its environment — i.e., it is structurally non-existent. A formula that gives 6.67 to a non-existent system is not a stability formula. It is a comforting lie.

### 4.2 Why the geometric mean is dual to information independence

Track B established that `D_F`, `D_P`, `D_A` are independent components of a complete description. Independence at the description level forces *non-compensatory aggregation* at the measurement level. The unique non-compensatory aggregator on three positive scalars in the closed form `(F · P · A)^(1/3)` — the geometric mean — has the property:

```
U = ∛(F · P · A)
```

with `U → 0` whenever any of F, P, A → 0. This is the structural dual of `D_F ⊕ D_P ⊕ D_A` independence: just as no two of the descriptions can substitute for the third, no two of the scores can compensate for the third in the aggregate.

Other aggregators (harmonic mean, minimum, more general power means with negative exponent) also have non-compensatory properties, but the geometric mean is canonical for two reasons:

1. It is the unique *symmetric* non-compensatory aggregator that respects scale-invariance — multiplying all three axes by a positive constant `k` multiplies `U` by `k`. Other non-compensatory aggregators do not preserve scale.
2. It is the *log-sum representation* of the three axes, dual to information-theoretic independence: `log(U) = (log F + log P + log A) / 3`. The log domain converts independent multiplicative structure into additive structure, exactly matching the `⊕` operator in Track B.

This is why U-Theory specifies `U = ∛(F · P · A)` and not `(F + P + A)/3`. The choice is not aesthetic. It is the unique aggregator dual to the structural independence forced by Tracks A, B, and C.

### 4.3 The aggregator and the lived data

The aggregator's correctness is not only formal. It matches the empirical pattern observed across realized stable systems:

- **Engineering systems** with one collapsed axis fail catastrophically, regardless of how strong the other two are. (A car with perfect engine and perfect brakes but no chassis fails.)
- **Organizations** with one collapsed axis fail commercially or institutionally. (A firm with great product and great execution but no market access fails.)
- **Living organisms** with one collapsed system (cardiovascular, respiratory, or nervous) die regardless of the other systems' strength.
- **Software architectures** with one collapsed pillar (security, reliability, or operational excellence) fail in production regardless of the other two pillars.

The geometric mean predicts these outcomes; the arithmetic mean does not. This is the empirical signature of `U = ∛(F · P · A)` as the correct aggregator across realized stable systems.

## 5. The Open Challenge Clause

UCT-1 is offered as a falsifiable claim. The challenge for any party disputing it is stated narrowly:

> **Challenge clause.** Produce one of the following:
>
> 1. A complete framework `T` for a class `C` of realized stable systems that uses fewer than three irreducible axes and survives Track A's case-exhaustion test (i.e., `T` answers the identity / context / effect questions without introducing a missing axis); **or**
>
> 2. A fourth irreducible component `D_X` of complete description that is not reducible to any combination of `D_F`, `D_P`, `D_A` and is necessary for completeness; **or**
>
> 3. A complete framework `T` that does not admit any faithful structure-preserving mapping into U-Theory's F/P/A spine (i.e., no consistent mapping of `T`'s axes to F/P/A is possible).

Producing any one of these defeats the corresponding track. Producing any two defeats UCT-1 as currently stated and forces a revision of the theorem's scope. Producing all three would refute the theorem entirely.

To date, none of the three has been produced and survived expert analysis. The challenge clause is open and standing.

This is what makes UCT-1 epistemically serious rather than merely rhetorical: it specifies, in advance, what would defeat it. The 2,500-year empirical record (§6) is consistent with no defeater having been found. The next year, the next decade, the next century may produce one. The theorem is offered for that test.

## 6. Empirical Convergence Catalogue

The catalogue below documents independent historical instances of triadic decomposition across at least eight major domains. Each entry is a framework that arose without contact with U-Theory (most predate U-Theory by centuries or millennia) and instantiates a triadic structure that admits a faithful mapping to F/P/A. This is the empirical content of UCT-1: the structural hypothesis predicts that complete frameworks over stable systems will instantiate the triad; the catalogue records that the pattern is observed.

### 6.0 Methodological Note on Catalogue Fits

Some entries in the catalogue are clean structural isomorphisms — Newtonian mechanics, MVC, REST, OpenTelemetry, and the Alibaba UModel all instantiate F/P/A as their *primary* and *exhaustive* design partition. Other entries are **interpretive fits** *[flagged below as such]* — frameworks where the author has surfaced the dominant triadic substructure within a richer system that has additional elements not reducible to F/P/A.

For example: Aristotle's *Poetics* identifies six elements (plot, character, thought, diction, melody, spectacle); the catalogue surfaces the first three as a dominant Plot/Character/Thought triad without claiming the other three reduce to it. Buddhism's Three Marks of Existence (Anicca / Dukkha / Anatta) is a doctrinal triad whose mapping to Action/Position/Form is interpretive — Anicca means *impermanence* rather than *action* per se, Dukkha means *unsatisfactoriness* rather than *position* per se, Anatta means *non-self* rather than *form* per se. The mappings are arguable, not exact.

This methodological honesty is itself UCT-1-consistent: the hypothesis predicts that *complete* frameworks will instantiate the triad as their *structural spine*, not that *every aspect* of every framework reduces to F/P/A. A framework can have a triadic spine plus additional domain-specific content without violating UCT-1; the hypothesis addresses only the spine. Entries flagged *[interpretive fit]* should be read as evidence of a triadic substructure within a richer framework, not as claims that the framework is exhaustively triadic.

The catalogue is therefore best understood as a mixed-strength corpus: the unflagged entries (~30) are clean structural isomorphisms; the flagged entries (~5) are interpretive readings offered for breadth, with their interpretive nature acknowledged. The joint convergence pattern is supported primarily by the clean entries; the flagged entries widen the historical scope without carrying the load.

## 7. The Probabilistic Argument

A frequent objection to convergence claims is that the overlap is generic. Many systems have three parts; many systems use schedulers; many systems talk about structure and context. Taken one feature at a time, no single resemblance is decisive.

The empirical record in §6 does not rest on any single resemblance. It rests on the joint signature.

### 7.1 The joint signature defined

For each framework in §6, the joint signature consists of:

1. *Exactly three* irreducible axes (not two, not four).
2. *Pairwise non-reducibility* of the axes — no two suffice to derive the third.
3. *Coverage of the F/P/A semantic categories* — identity / context / effect.
4. *Domain-independent applicability* — the framework claims to work across instances of its target class.

Any single feature is common; the joint signature is rare.

### 7.2 The joint-signature argument (qualitative)

A naive multiplication of feature probabilities — e.g., `(1/5)^4 ≈ 1.6×10⁻³` per framework, raised to the power of fifty frameworks for a vanishingly small joint probability — would be **fallacious in this case**, because the four signature features defined in §7.1 are highly correlated. A framework that uses *exactly three* irreducible axes (feature 1) almost necessarily satisfies *pairwise non-reducibility* (feature 2) — these are nearly the same property under different vocabulary. Multiplying correlated probabilities produces misleadingly small numbers and would be dismissed by any trained statistician on a single read.

The serious form of the argument is therefore not quantitative but qualitative. The convergence cannot be plausibly explained by chance because of three structural properties of the empirical record:

1. **The joint signature is distinctive.** Of all the possible structural decompositions a framework could choose — binary, ternary, quaternary, n-ary, lattice, hierarchy, network, tagged-feature-set — the *constrained ternary with non-reducibility* pattern is one of many available options. There is no a priori reason a framework should land on it absent some structural pressure.
2. **The joint signature recurs under isolation.** It appears across mutually isolated traditions — Hellenic philosophy, Indian Trimurti, Chinese San Cai, modern physics, software engineering — that did not exchange methods for centuries or millennia. Independent emergence of the same minor structural choice across isolated traditions is not what coincidence looks like.
3. **The joint signature is predictive.** When a new domain matures to formal completeness, it instantiates the same pattern under different vocabulary. The Alibaba UModel observability case (October 2025, documented in `ARTICLE_U_THEORY_PRIOR_ART_ALIBABA_UMODEL.md`) is a recent industrial example: enterprise-scale IT observability, with no public evidence presently available that its design was derived from U-Theory, instantiated EntitySet/EntitySetLink/Field — a clean F/P/A spine — under independent design pressure.

The recurrence under isolation, combined with the predictive accuracy under new domains, is the structural signature of an underlying constraint — not a quantifiable probability. UCT-1 names that constraint as completeness-forced minimal partition; the catalogue (§6) records its empirical signature; the open challenge clause (§5) keeps the hypothesis falsifiable.

### 7.3 What the joint-signature argument establishes

The argument does *not* prove that the triad is the *correct* minimum partition — Tracks A and B (and the Track C analogy) carry that load, each with its own epistemic limits acknowledged in §2.4. What the argument establishes is that the *observed* convergence pattern is highly distinctive, recurrent across isolated traditions, and predictive of new cases — three properties that are not jointly explained by random independent emergence. ## Appendix B — Methodological Notes

### B.1 On the choice of axis labels

The labels "Form," "Position," "Action" are chosen for two reasons: (i) they are sufficiently abstract to apply across all domains in §6 without forcing domain-specific meaning, and (ii) they correspond etymologically to the three minimal questions of stable existence: *what continues?* (Form), *where is it distinguishable?* (Position), *what does it do?* (Action). Other label sets (Identity / Context / Effect; Structure / Place / Process; etc.) are equivalent under the terminal-object morphism of Track C.

### B.2 On the triad's relationship to other minimum partitions

The triad is the minimum partition for *complete description of realized stable systems*. It is not the minimum partition for every conceivable problem. Some problems (e.g., signal vs noise) are correctly modeled with two axes; some (e.g., color vision) are correctly modeled with three but with different axis semantics; some problems are inherently higher-dimensional. UCT-1's claim is restricted to the class of realized stable systems as defined in §1.2.

### B.3 On the non-compensatory aggregator

The geometric mean is one of several non-compensatory aggregators. Section §4.2 explains why it is canonical (scale-invariance plus log-sum representation). Other aggregators that respect non-compensation (minimum, harmonic mean, generalized mean with negative exponent) can be used in domain-specific applications without violating UCT-1's structural claim, but the geometric mean is the reference operator for cross-domain comparison.
